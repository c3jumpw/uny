What a backend is actually doing

Every app — a booking tool, a customer portal, an inventory system — needs the same handful of things working underneath it before it does anything useful. Strip away the jargon and it comes down to five jobs:

DATABASE

Somewhere to keep the data. Every customer, order, and record needs a home that doesn't lose information or slow down as you grow.

AUTH

Somewhere to check who's who. Customers and staff need to sign in safely, with the right access for the right person.

STORAGE

Somewhere to put files. Photos, PDFs, receipts, exports — files need a home that's fast to upload to and fast to pull from.

REALTIME

Somewhere to broadcast change. When one person updates something, everyone else looking at it should see it happen without refreshing.

FUNCTIONS

Somewhere for logic to run. Sending a receipt, calculating a total, checking a rule — work that happens automatically, not by hand.

Why businesses end up overpaying for this

Most teams end up assembling these five pieces from different vendors — a database provider here, an auth service there, a separate file host, a separate realtime add-on. Each one gets its own bill, its own dashboard, and its own login. Someone still has to make sure they all work together and stay online.

The real cost isn't any one service. It's the time your team spends gluing them together, and re-gluing them every time something changes.
